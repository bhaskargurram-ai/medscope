### 1.4 Unpublished but influential (arXiv only as of 2026-11-14 to my knowledge; do not cite as peer-reviewed)
- **MedHEval** (Chang et al., arXiv 2503.02157, 2025). Groups Med-LVLM hallucinations as visual misinterpretation, knowledge deficiency or context misalignment. Tests 11 models and 7 mitigation methods and finds the mitigations have "limited effectiveness". This is the closest published-style precedent for MedScope's negative result.
- **Med-HallMark / MediHall Score / MediHallDetector** (Chen et al., arXiv 2406.10185, 2024).
- **EchoBench** (Yuan et al., arXiv 2509.20146, 2025). Sycophancy in medical LVLMs: Claude 3.7 Sonnet 45.98%, GPT-4.1 59.15%, many medical models >95%. Negative, one-shot and few-shot prompting reduce it.
- **Benchmarking and Mitigating Sycophancy in Medical VLMs / VIPER** (Xu et al., arXiv 2509.21979, 2025). Authority and mimicry cues trigger sycophancy. VIPER filters out cues that are not backed by evidence.
- **MedCFVQA** (arXiv 2505.16209, 2025). Causal modality-preference debiasing for Med-VQA. Venue not confirmed.
- **"To Agree or To Be Right? The Grounding-Sycophancy Tradeoff in Medical VLMs"** (arXiv 2603.22623, 2026) and **"Position, Not Provenance ..."** (arXiv 2607.27304, 2026). Titles only seen; not read.

## 3. Gaps that no published work in this review fills (what MedScope could own)

1. **Matched-control counterfactual evaluation of presence claims.** HEAL-VPT (region swap), MMedPO (lesion noise), CCIS-MVQA (LRP-based feature counterfactuals) and RadEdit (diffusion edits for classifiers) all remove evidence. None pairs each removal with an **equal-shaped edit elsewhere** to subtract the effect of the edit itself. ESG is, as far as I found, the first artefact-controlled measure of evidence sensitivity for VLM presence answers. Balık 2026 makes the closest argument (mismatched-image beats blank-image control), but only at whole-image level.
2. **Reader-validated counterfactuals.** No VLM-hallucination paper has physicians adjudicate whether the edited image still shows the finding. MedScope's finding that readers still see a finding on ~40% of non-colonoscopy erasures is itself new: it quantifies the label noise in every counterfactual-image benchmark, including HEAL's VPT.
3. **Generalisation to a held-out editor.** No paper tests whether counterfactual-trained gains survive a different inpainter or editor. This is the obvious shortcut (editor fingerprint) and no one has measured it.
4. **Five modalities, one protocol.** Existing image-side tests are CXR-only (HEAL, RadEdit) or use VQA-RAD/SLAKE (MMedPO). Colonoscopy, breast US and brain MRI have no evidence-removal test.
5. **Pre-registered negative results across method families.** MedHEval (arXiv) reports that mitigations are weak, but no peer-reviewed paper pre-registers DPO, GRPO, consistency losses, encoder LoRA, multi-agent and 270 decision policies against one causal metric. This negative map is publishable in its own right (NeurIPS D&B / MIDL / MedIA).
6. **Separating representation failure from decision failure.** No medical paper reports whether the encoder representation even *separates* erased from control views (probe AUROC) before blaming the LLM head. MediConfusion hints at encoder confusion, but on natural pairs.
7. **Abstention judged by evidence sensitivity.** Wienholt 2026 (DSE) and CARES judge abstention by retained-set accuracy. No one asks whether abstention removes *unsupported* claims more than supported ones, i.e., ESG among answered cases.

## 4. Rival explanations for why no training or inference method raised ESG beyond SFT on counterfactual views

| ID | Rival explanation | Supporting literature | Discriminating prediction (test) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Label ceiling / ambiguous targets.** Outside colonoscopy, ~40% of erasures still show a finding to physicians. "Absent" is the wrong target for many erased items, so any method that learns better evidence use also "claims" more on them, and the gains cancel | Tanno 2024 (reader variance); RadEdit (unintended edit content) | Recompute ESG on **reader-clean erasures only** (colonoscopy + reader-clean others), or weight by reader probability. If methods separate on the clean subset but not overall, R1 holds. If they tie there too, R1 is rejected |
| R2 | **Editor-fingerprint shortcut.** SFT learns "this inpainter's texture at a lesion-plausible location means absent". Control edits share the fingerprint but sit at less plausible locations, so the model learns *location × artefact*, which does not transfer to a new inpainter | Cheng 2025 (VLMs barely notice artefacts, so whatever they learn is fragile); RadEdit | (a) Linear probe on vision tokens: erased-vs-control AUROC high for the training inpainter, near chance for the held-out one. (b) **Hard controls**: place control edits at anatomically lesion-plausible sites. If ESG(train-inpainter) collapses with hard controls, R2 holds |
| R3 | **Residual peri-lesional evidence.** Lesion-shaped erasure leaves oedema, mass effect, acoustic shadowing or vascular cues, so a claim can be correct reasoning (physicians also see it) | CIMB-MVQA (visual co-occurrence confounders); Huppertz (context reliance) | Erase with dilated masks (lesion + 1-3 lesion radii) and matched dilated controls. If claim rates on erasures fall monotonically with dilation for *all* models while ESG differences between methods stay at zero, R3 explains much of the floor |
| R4 | **Question/base-rate prior.** Training questions are asked mostly when the finding is present, so the decoder's P(yes \| question) dominates and every fine-tune inherits it | ProbMed; SurgCheck; Schramm; Dietrich; Zhan / DeCoCT / CEDO (CP splits) | Measure P(yes \| question, mismatched lesion-free image of the same modality) per Balık 2026. If this is high and tracks P(claim \| erased) across methods (r > 0.7), R4 holds. The question-blind reader failing argues against pure R4, so R4 likely interacts with R5 |
| R5 | **Encoder or resolution bottleneck.** The 4B model's vision tokens do not separate erased from original or control at the lesion, so no head-side method (DPO, GRPO, arbiter) can create ESG | MediConfusion; ProbMed ablation (visual understanding is the bottleneck); LobA (highlighting helps) | Probe AUROC(original vs erased) on frozen vision tokens pooled inside the lesion mask. If ≤ 0.6 on held-out-inpainter erasures, the bottleneck is representational and F4 is required. If ≥ 0.8 while ESG is flat, the bottleneck is the decision path (go to F2/F3) |
| R6 | **Threshold shift, not discrimination.** All 8 methods move P(claim) on erased and control views together (a calibration shift), which is why only abstention changed utility | Wienholt 2026 (gain via abstention); MedHEval (arXiv) | Compute AUROC of P(present) for erased-vs-control (and original-vs-erased) per method. If AUROC is identical within CI across methods while operating points differ, R6 holds: the methods change the cut-off, not the evidence |
| R7 | **Off-manifold fallback.** Erased images are out of distribution; under uncertainty the decoder falls back to the prior "yes" | CARES (no abstention under OOD); Cheng 2025 | Within erased items, P(claim) should correlate positively with an OOD score (e.g., encoder Mahalanobis distance or reconstruction error) after controlling for reader judgement |
| R8 | **Power / estimator variance.** Held-out-inpainter test sets are too small to detect ESG changes of 3-5 points | Not specific to this literature | Bootstrap CIs on ESG and minimum detectable effect. If MDE exceeds every method's effect on the training inpainter, the "failure" is not shown |
| R9 | **Preference objectives ignored the image.** DPO/GRPO pairs that differ only in text let the model satisfy the objective without looking | mDPO (2B, unverified venue); MMedPO (image-side negatives help) | Re-run DPO with *image-conditional* pairs (same answer, original vs erased image), as in mDPO/MMedPO. If ESG rises only in this variant, the earlier DPO failure was a design choice, not a dead end |

Note: R1 and R5/R6 are the cheapest and most informative to test first. They decide whether the next method should target labels, representation or decision.

## 5. Candidate method frameworks

### F1. Counterfactual Evidence Mediation (CEM): train the answer to be *mediated* by a self-localised region under randomised erasure operators
- **Mechanism.** The model emits an evidence region (box or `<SEG>` mask, as in LobA) before the yes/no answer. During training, each lesion image generates, on the fly: (i) erasure of the *model's own* evidence region and (ii) an equal-shape control erasure elsewhere. Both use a fill operator sampled from a pool (≥3 inpainters + blur + mean-fill + patch-shuffle + noise), i.e., domain randomisation of the editor. Losses: CE on the original; a "necessity" loss pushing P(present) down after evidence erasure (soft-weighted by reader-estimated cleanliness, to address R1); an "invariance" loss keeping P(present) unchanged after control erasure; a segmentation loss on GT masks when available.
- **Why it could work where MedScope failed.** The earlier paired-view consistency loss used fixed erasures from one inpainter, which invites R2. Randomising the fill operator removes any single fingerprint, and erasing the *model's* region ties the answer to where it looks rather than to where the dataset editor edited.
- **Closest work and difference.** LobA/HEAL (IJCAI 2025) localises and highlights but never trains on erasure. CCIS-MVQA (TMI 2024) erases LRP regions in feature space with a classic backbone and no control. MMedPO (ICML 2025) uses lesion noise in DPO with no control and no operator randomisation. CRG (ECCV 2024) is inference-only. **New here:** matched control + operator randomisation + reader-weighted necessity + self-localised region in an LLM-scale VLM.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** On **held-out-inpainter** erasures, ESG(CEM) − ESG(SFT-counterfactual) ≥ +5 points (95% CI excludes 0), with a *smaller* gain on the training inpainter (the gain comes from removing the fingerprint). If the held-out gain is not larger than the in-distribution gain, R2 was not the issue.
- **Compute (1 A100-80GB).** Qwen-4B + LoRA (r = 16-64), ~20-30k lesion images × (1 original + 2 erasures + 1 control) per step. Classical fills are cheap; diffusion inpainting should be precomputed (≈0.5-1 s/img per inpainter → ~1 GPU-day for 3 inpainters × 30k). Training ≈ 1-2 GPU-days. Total ≈ 3 A100-days.

### F2. Self-Counterfactual Evidence Test (SCET): turn each presence answer into a calibrated hypothesis test against matched-control nulls
- **Mechanism.** At inference, localise the candidate region (model's own region or segmenter), produce k erasures of that region and k matched-shape control erasures with randomised fill operators, and score s = logit_present(original) − mean logit_present(region-erased). Compare s with the null distribution from control erasures and calibrate a split-conformal threshold on a calibration set. Answer "present" only if the evidence statistic rejects the null at level α; otherwise answer "absent" or "refer" (three-way).
- **Why it could work where MedScope failed.** The 270-policy screen combined *scores* (votes, TTA, crops, token probabilities, segmenter scores). None of those asks whether removing the evidence changes the answer *more than* removing something equal-sized elsewhere. That test is ESG at item level. It also gives an abstention rule judged by evidence sensitivity rather than entropy (gap 7).
- **Closest work and difference.** CRG (ECCV 2024) contrasts with/without a region, but with blackout, no control null and no calibration. VCD/Med-VCD use global distortions. Wienholt 2026 DSE abstains on semantic entropy. The MedScope multi-agent "erase/control verifier" judged *given* images; SCET *generates* counterfactuals of each test image and tests the model's sensitivity to them.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** At matched coverage (e.g., 80% answered), ESG among answered held-out-inpainter items is ≥ 8 points higher under SCET than under DSE-style entropy abstention and the best of the 270 policies, while sensitivity on real lesions stays ≥ 90%. If SCET only matches entropy abstention, the model's logits carry no lesion-local evidence, which points back to R5.
- **Compute.** Inference only: (2k+1) forward passes per item. With k = 4, 9 passes × ~0.15 s on a 4B model ≈ 1.4 s/item. Fills are classical or cached. A 10k-item evaluation ≈ 4 GPU-hours. Calibration is trivial.

### F3. Prior-Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (POFT): remove the question-prior direction from the gradient and the logit
- **Mechanism.** Train a cheap question-only branch (same LoRA backbone with a blank or mismatched lesion-free image, per Balık) that predicts P(yes | question, modality). During SFT on counterfactual views, (a) project out the component of each LoRA gradient aligned with the prior branch's gradient (as in DE-CaGI and CEDO's orthogonal gradients), and (b) at inference subtract the prior branch's natural direct effect from the logit (as in CF-VQA's TIE).
- **Why it could work where MedScope failed.** If R4 holds, every method so far inherited the same prior. Removing it explicitly changes the *direction* of learning rather than the loss weighting, which DPO and the cost-matrix GRPO did not do.
- **Closest work and difference.** DE-CaGI (MedIA 2026), CEDO (IJCAI 2025), DeCoCT (MICCAI 2025) and CF-VQA (CVPR 2021) are all evaluated on changed-prior answer splits with classic or small backbones, never on image counterfactuals or LLM-scale LoRA. New here: prior estimated with mismatched-image controls; evaluation by ESG.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** P(yes | mismatched lesion-free image) drops ≥ 50% relative, and ESG rises ≥ 4 points on held-out-inpainter erasures. **If the prior drops but ESG does not move**, R4 is ruled out as the binding constraint. That is an informative negative result.
- **Compute.** About 1.3× SFT cost (one extra forward/backward for the prior branch). ≈ 1-1.5 A100-days.

### F4. Lesion-Token Counterfactual Contrastive Adaptation (LTCA): fix the representation first, then the head
- **Mechanism.** Stage 1: adapt the vision encoder and projector with a token-level contrastive objective on triplets (original, erased, control) under randomised fill operators. Lesion-mask tokens of original vs erased must differ (margin), while tokens of original vs control must match everywhere and lesion tokens must match under control. Add a linear "evidence probe" whose held-out-inpainter AUROC serves as the stopping criterion. Stage 2: standard SFT of the LLM LoRA on counterfactual views with the adapted encoder frozen.
- **Why it could work where MedScope failed.** Vision-encoder LoRA was trained only through the answer loss, a weak and long gradient path. LTCA supervises the representation directly where R5 says the information is lost. The probe tells you *before* LLM training whether evidence can be decoded at all.
- **Closest work and difference.** MediConfusion diagnoses encoder confusion but offers no fix. LobA highlights patches but does not train their content. MMedPO's lesion noise acts only at the preference level. RadEdit stress-tests but does not train. New here: token-level counterfactual contrast with matched control and operator randomisation, gated by a probe.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** Probe AUROC (original vs erased, held-out inpainter) rises from baseline to ≥ 0.85, and ESG then rises ≥ 5 points. **If probe AUROC rises but ESG does not**, the LLM ignores available evidence; switch to F2/F3.
- **Compute.** Encoder-only stage (~0.4-0.6B ViT) on ~30k triplets × 3-5 epochs ≈ 0.5-1 A100-day; Stage 2 SFT ≈ 1 day. Total ≈ 2 A100-days.

### F5 (recommended unifying frame). "Diagnose-then-target": a pre-registered decision tree over F1-F4
Run R1 (reader-clean subset), R5 (probe AUROC) and R6 (AUROC vs operating point) first; each costs under 2 GPU-hours. Then:
- Probe low → F4.
- Probe high and AUROC flat → F2 (+F3 if the mismatched-image prior is high).
- Probe high and AUROC high but ESG flat → R1/R3 label problem: report reader-weighted ESG and train F1 with reader-weighted necessity.

This turns the eight failures into a mechanistic finding ("evidence is lost at stage X") and gives the paper **one method** (F1+F2: train with operator-randomised self-erasure, decide with a matched-control evidence test) that is new against LobA, MMedPO, CRG and DSE. Total budget ≈ 5-6 A100-days.

## 6. Key takeaways for the MedScope paper
- **Novelty is defensible.** The nearest neighbours are HEAL-MedVQA's VPT (region swap, CXR, no control), MMedPO (lesion noise as DPO negatives, accuracy-only evaluation), RadEdit (editing for classifiers) and Balık 2026 (whole-image mismatched control). None combines a lesion-shaped erasure, a matched control edit, reader validation, a held-out editor and multiple modalities.
- **Must-cite clinical support.** Strotzer (Radiology 2024: GPT-4V specificity 8-52% on healthy controls), Huppertz (Eur Radiol: 258 fabricated findings in 412 responses), Schramm (Radiology 2025), Dietrich (Radiology: AI 2026), Clusmann (NEJM AI 2025), Jin (npj DM 2024).
- **Must-include baselines.** LobA-style localise-then-answer, MMedPO/mDPO-style image-conditional DPO, VCD/Med-VCD/CRG decoding, DSE abstention (Wienholt 2026).
- **Reviewer-facing risk.** HEAL-MedVQA is the paper reviewers will cite as "already done". Pre-empt it with the control-edit argument and by reporting how HEAL-style VPT changes when a matched control is added.
