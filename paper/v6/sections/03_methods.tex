\section{Materials and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study design and predictions}
\label{subsec:design}

The study varies the image and holds the question fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}). Each test case is an image with an expert lesion mask and a yes/no question whose answer depends on that lesion, for example ``Is there a polyp in this image?''. In the \emph{erased} image the lesion region is filled in by inpainting. In the \emph{control edit} the same tool fills a region of the same shape elsewhere in the image and leaves the lesion untouched. Unedited \emph{lesion-free} images of the same imaging type receive the same kind of question. We call the test images and their scoring MedScope-Bench.
% src: brief sections 3 and 4; v5 P473-479

A model whose answer depends on the lesion should report the finding on the original image, answer ``absent'' (or refer) on the erased image, and still report it after the control edit. A model that answers from the question or the surrounding anatomy answers all three alike, and one that answers ``absent'' to any trace of editing does so on both edited images. The evidence sensitivity gap (ESG; Section~\ref{subsec:measures}) is zero for both and for any constant answer. Because reporting nothing also avoids reporting erased lesions, the share of lesion images answered correctly is given with every ESG.
% src: brief section 4 (sentence 2); v5 P498; L229-238

The design measures whether the answer changes when the lesion region is filled in, not whether the finding has gone: an erasure may leave part of the lesion or its secondary signs. Physicians therefore read a sample of the images without being told which were edited (Section~\ref{subsec:readers}), and a second design, with no edit, pairs an unedited image that shows a lesion with one of the same patient that does not (Section~\ref{subsec:pairs}). Table~\ref{tab:overview} lists the questions in the order of the Results.
% src: brief section 4 (sentences 5-7); RE193-197

\subsection{Data, splits and leakage controls}
\label{subsec:data}

We chose sources that release expert lesion masks and lesion-free images of the same imaging type; training, validation and the main test come from five public sources (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}). Lesions under 20 pixels or over 20\% of the image and 623 diffuse chest X-ray findings were excluded, so the test covers focal lesions that can be erased. The lesion-free images of a source are not always from the same anatomical sites as its lesion images (in colonoscopy they are HyperKvasir frames of normal landmarks), which affects lesion-free claims and net utility but not ESG, a comparison of two edits of one image. On 26 of the 288 lesion-free test images at least seven of the nine comparison models reported a finding, so some lesion-free claims may be correct readings.
% src: v5 P473, P620; L272 (623 diffuse findings); supp training details (HyperKvasir landmarks; v5 P620); L606-609 (26 of 288 disputed by >= 7 of 9)

Every image was assigned to its split before any view was built. Splits were made by patient where the source releases patient identifiers (ChestX-Det, TCGA-LGG) and otherwise by group of near-identical images; because two dissimilar images of one patient can then fall in different splits, an analysis removes the test cases most similar to any training image (Section~\ref{subsec:res_trained}). A hash comparison of 6,849 unedited images found no shared patient or group identifier and one image shared across sets, a validation image that is also a fresh external film (\ref{sec:s_data}). Thresholds, selection rules and fitted quantities used training or validation data only.
% src: v5 P622, P624; RE117-125; L844-858 (six sets, 6,849 images, one shared image); L439-440 (SIIM shares images with NIH ChestX-ray14)

The main test holds 507 cases from 416 patients or image groups (219 with a lesion mask, 288 lesion-free) and 1,115 views: 219 each of original, erased and control-edited images, 288 lesion-free images and 170 degraded copies, on which the expected answer is a request for a better image. Development versions of the trained model had been evaluated on the main test and on four external datasets not used in training (169 cases), and the training of \ms{} was changed in response to their weaknesses; their number was not recorded (\ref{sec:s_training}). We therefore added two sets on which no model had been evaluated before \ms{} was fixed: a fresh internal set (76 cases from unused ChestX-Det patients) and a fresh external set (316 cases from four further datasets). Test-set sizes were set by the available cases, not by a power calculation.

\subsection{Erased, control-edited and lesion-free views}
\label{subsec:views}

The erased image is the original with the lesion mask, dilated by 5 pixels, filled by the inpainting model LaMa \citep{suvorov2022lama}. The control edit leaves the lesion untouched and fills a region of the same shape, with the same tool, in tissue elsewhere, so no lesion pixel changes; it matches the erasure in every property except the removal of the lesion region. Lesion-free images are unedited and carry the same expected answer as an erased image, so a model that answers ``absent'' only when it detects an edit fails on them.
% src: v5 P476-479

Training used two editing tools, LaMa and tissue transplant (tissue from the same image blended over the lesion). TELEA \citep{telea2004image} and Stable Diffusion inpainting \citep{rombach2022high} were not used in training, and the main-test erasures and control edits were remade with each on the same masks. An \emph{edit-site control} on validation images fills a ring around the intact lesion, starting 2 pixels outside it; the same analysis erased half of the mask, or pasted the lesion pixels back into the erased image.
% src: v5 P482, P484; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (data); L1250-1255 (ring, half and restored erasures, FRAMEWORK_STAGE0.md)

Edited views passed automated checks and were not selected by hand (\ref{sec:s_views}). For the blank-image control, 183 main-test lesion cases were shown as a uniform grey image with the same question. Erasures that a trained edit detector scored no higher than the median control edit are called \emph{low-visibility}. Questions are \emph{finding-specific} when they name a finding (114 lesion cases) and \emph{generic} otherwise (105).
% src: v5 P486; RE258-268, RE423-445; L1416-1417; L1766, L1786

\subsection{Outcome measures}
\label{subsec:measures}

The scoring code read each final answer from its action tag (a reply without one was read as a plain answer; \ref{sec:s_prompts}) and classed it as \emph{present}, \emph{absent}, \emph{refer} (the image is usable but the model does not decide) or a request for a better image. A lesion is \emph{denied} when the answer is absent or refer. The primary measure is the evidence sensitivity gap,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:esg}
\mathrm{ESG} = \Pr(\text{denied} \mid \text{erased}) - \Pr(\text{denied} \mid \text{control edit}).
\end{equation}
ESG is zero for any constant answer and for any model that responds to the two edits alike. It is a Youden index for the paired contrast: the share of erased images denied plus the share of control-edited images not denied, minus one.
% src: v5 P490-498; L261-262 (replies without an action tag read as plain answers), L365-370

\emph{Erased-lesion claims} are the share of erased images answered present, and \emph{lesion-free claims} the share of unedited lesion-free images answered present. \emph{Lesion images correct} is the share of original and control-edited images answered correctly.
% src: v5 P500

\emph{Net utility} is the mean reward per view under a cost matrix that we wrote and did not elicit from clinicians (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_cost}): a correct answer earns $+1$ ($+0.25$ on a degraded copy), a wrong answer $-2$ ($-4$ on a lesion image whose finding we marked in advance as critical), a referral $+0.5$ on erased and lesion-free images and $-0.3$ on lesion images, and a request for a better image $+0.5$ on a degraded copy and $-0.3$ or $-0.5$ elsewhere. A correct box on a lesion image adds $+0.5$, each tool call beyond the first costs $0.05$, an unreadable output scores $-1$, and erased and lesion-free images are scored alike, so detecting an edit earns nothing. \ms{} was trained with this reward (Section~\ref{subsec:models}) and the comparison models were not shown it, so net utility favours the trained model by construction; ESG and the claim rates do not depend on the matrix.
% src: v5 P500-503, P513-515; configs/reward.yaml as given in Supplementary Table tab:s_cost; L1702-1703 (critical findings); v5 P545-547 (stage 2 keeps the highest-reward sampled answer)

For unedited pairs, the \emph{natural ESG} is the share of lesion-free images denied minus the share of lesion images of the same patients denied, so a request for a better image on a lesion image counts as not denied. For chest films we report the \emph{Youden index} \citep{youden1950index}, the share of pneumothorax films answered present plus the share of films without pneumothorax denied, minus one, which counts that request as a miss. The reference standard is the expert mask or label of the source dataset; for an erased image it is absence by construction, which the readers assess. The primary outcomes, fixed before \ms{} was trained, are ESG, erased-lesion claims and net utility on the main test.
% src: v5 P606, P659; RE215-216, RE512-513; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (primary outcomes)

\subsection{Reader study}
\label{subsec:readers}

The specialist round is the primary reader assessment (protocol in \ref{sec:s_readers}). \emph{Images:} 212 main-test images from different cases, 36 of each kind: unedited lesion and lesion-free images, and erasures and control edits made with LaMa and with Stable Diffusion (32 Stable Diffusion control edits). \emph{Readers:} board-certified radiologists with at least five years of practice after certification, and a board-certified gastroenterologist for colonoscopy images; none had read in the earlier round. \emph{Independence:} each reader worked alone, without labels, kinds of edit, model outputs or other readers' answers; readers who complete the reading are offered authorship, and the gastroenterologist is an author. \emph{Presentation:} one image at a time with its clinical question, in a shuffled order; readers were told only that the study concerns automatically edited images. \emph{Questions:} whether the image had been digitally edited, and the clinical question (yes, no or cannot tell). \emph{Criterion:} for each editing tool, the erasures are accepted when the share of erased images that the reader majority calls present has an exact 95\% upper limit of at most 10\%; the gastroenterologist's answers count for colonoscopy only. The round closes when two radiologists have read all images or on a fixed date.
% src: facts E; RE77-93, RE140-187; R5_EXPERT_READERS.md (message to readers); supp_a reader protocol (authorship offer); 07_backmatter.tex CRediT (S.C.G. specialist reader)

An earlier round used two trainee physicians (internal medicine and radiology; one is an author), 128 main-test images (32 each of lesion images, LaMa erasures, LaMa control edits and lesion-free images) and the same questions and criterion; the model answers were then re-scored on the erasures that neither trainee called present.
% src: facts E; READER_VERIFIED.md; v5 P1552; 07_backmatter.tex CRediT (J.C. trainee reader)

\subsection{Models}
\label{subsec:models}

Every model answered under one protocol (\ref{sec:s_prompts}, with access dates and settings): the image, with its long side capped at 768 pixels, the question and the same system prompt, which addresses the model as MedScope. It could make up to three tool calls (enlarge a region, adjust contrast) and ended with an answer (with an optional box), a referral or a request for a better image. A failed request was repeated up to six times and then scored as unreadable; the share of unreadable outputs was not recorded for the final runs.

\subsection{Unedited image pairs}
\label{subsec:pairs}

REAL-Colon \citep{biffi2024realcolon} holds 60 colonoscopy videos from four centres, with a box on every frame that shows a resected polyp. For each of a video's three longest runs of boxed frames, a polyp frame from the middle of the run was paired with a polyp-free frame (no box marked) of the same video, 10 to 30~s from the run and at least 10~s from any boxed frame (\ref{sec:s_views}). Both frames received the question ``Is there a polyp in this image?'', which gave 103 pairs from 46 videos; 96 pairs also received a \emph{context-matched} polyp-free frame, 3 to 10~s from the nearest boxed frame. LDPolypVideo \citep{ma2021ldpolyp} holds 160 annotated clips from one hospital on which no model of this study had been trained, selected or tested; with the pairing rules expressed in frames, 116 clips gave a pair.
% src: v5 P604, P608; RE199-205, RE349-352; L1646; RE573-586, L2067-2070 (LDPolypVideo)

Chest films were not paired by patient: pneumothorax detection was tested on the 200 new SIIM-ACR patients \citep{siim2019pneumothorax} and on the 600 CheXthought test films \citep{sharma2026chexthought}, whose labels combine the CheXpert Plus label \citep{chambon2024chexpertplus} with our rule-based reading of radiologists' statements. Same-patient follow-up pairs from NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestx} are a secondary analysis (\ref{sec:s_views}).

\subsection{Analysis plan and statistics}
\label{subsec:stats}

The analysis plan was written during the study, each rule after the results that preceded it. It is our own dated record in the public project repository (\url{https://github.com/bhaskargurram-ai/medscope}), not a third-party registry; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s_overview} and~\ref{tab:s_plan} give every analysis with its date, status, rule and outcome. An analysis is \emph{pre-specified} when its test set, comparison and decision rule were fixed and dated before the data it applies to were seen, \emph{descriptive} when it was fixed in advance without a decision rule, and \emph{post hoc} otherwise. Confirmatory analyses, in which no model of this study had been run on the test data when the rule was dated, were \ms{} on the fresh internal and fresh external sets and on the REAL-Colon pairs, \msn{} on the LDPolypVideo pairs, the first test on the new SIIM-ACR patients and the two reader rounds. Other analyses with a dated rule are secondary, and four of them re-analyse main-test answers whose pooled results were known. Post hoc analyses, among them the models \msp{} and \msn{} and the family of 27 primary comparisons, are marked where reported.

\emph{Primary comparisons:} \ms{} against each of the nine comparison models on the three primary outcomes, 27 comparisons, each tested at a Bonferroni-corrected level of $0.05/27$. \emph{Other comparisons:} two-sided at the 0.05 level, uncorrected, read with their intervals. \emph{Intervals:} 95\% confidence intervals (CI) by percentile bootstrap from 2,000 resamples. \emph{Resampled unit:} the case on the edited test sets (all views of a case together), the video or clip for colonoscopy pairs and the patient for chest films (patients or image groups in a sensitivity analysis; \ref{sec:s_stats}). \emph{Comparisons between models:} paired, with both models scored on the same resampled units; the $p$ value is twice the smaller share of resampled differences on either side of zero. With 2,000 resamples the smallest non-zero $p$ value is 0.001, so the primary conclusions rest on the paired intervals. \emph{Reader proportions:} exact binomial intervals. \emph{What an interval reflects:} the sampling of test cases for one trained model and one run of each comparison model. Variation between training seeds is reported separately, as the mean and standard deviation (SD) over three seeds, and the two are never combined. \emph{Software:} Python with NumPy for the analyses; PyTorch 2.11, Transformers 5.22.1 and PEFT 0.21.2 for \ms; PyTorch 2.14.1, Transformers 5.24.1 and PEFT 0.22.0 for the final runs of \msn.
