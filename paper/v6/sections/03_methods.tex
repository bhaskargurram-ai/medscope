\section{Materials and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study design and predictions}
\label{subsec:design}

The study varies the image and holds the question fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}). Each test case is an image with an expert lesion mask and a closed question whose answer depends on that lesion, for example ``Is there a polyp in this image?''. In the \emph{erased} image the lesion is removed by inpainting. In the \emph{control edit} the same tool fills a region of the same shape elsewhere in the image and leaves the lesion untouched. Unedited \emph{lesion-free} images of the same imaging type receive the same kind of question. We call the test images and their scoring MedScope-Bench.
% src: brief sections 3 and 4; v5 P473-479

A model that uses the image should report the finding on the original image, answer that it is absent (or refer the case) on the erased image, and still report it after the control edit. A model that does not use the image, because it answers from the wording of the question or from the surrounding anatomy, answers all three views alike. A model that treats any trace of editing as a sign of absence denies the finding on both edited views. The primary measure, the evidence sensitivity gap (ESG; Section~\ref{subsec:measures}), is zero for both of these models and for any constant answer. A model can also avoid reporting erased lesions by reporting nothing, so the share of real lesions found is given with every ESG.
% src: brief section 4 (sentence 2); v5 P498; L229-238

An erasure may leave part of the lesion or its secondary signs, and an erased image is still an edited image. Specialist readers therefore assessed the images without being told which were edited (Section~\ref{subsec:readers}). A second design uses no edit: an unedited image that shows a lesion is paired with an unedited image of the same patient that does not (Section~\ref{subsec:pairs}), and a model that uses the image should report the finding on the first image only. Table~\ref{tab:overview} lists every question of the study, the design element that answers it, the test data, the status of the analysis and where the result is reported.

\subsection{Data, splits and leakage controls}
\label{subsec:data}

Sources were chosen because they release expert lesion masks, which are needed to erase a lesion and to place a control edit of the same shape, and lesion-free images of the same imaging type. Training, validation and the main test come from five public sources: SLAKE (CT and MRI of several body regions), Kvasir-SEG with lesion-free frames from HyperKvasir (colonoscopy), ChestX-Det (chest X-ray), BUSI (breast ultrasound) and TCGA-LGG (brain MRI) (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}). Lesions smaller than 20 pixels or covering more than 20\% of the image were excluded, because they cannot be erased and checked reliably.
% src: v5 P473, P620

Three controls guard against leakage between training and test data. First, every image was assigned to its split before any view was built, so all views of an image stay in one split. Second, splits were made by patient where the source releases patient identifiers (ChestX-Det, TCGA-LGG) and otherwise by group of near-identical images. In the other three sources two dissimilar images of one patient can therefore fall in different splits; a pre-specified analysis removes the test cases most similar to any training image (Section~\ref{subsec:res_alternatives}). Third, all 6,849 unedited images were hashed and compared across sets: no case, patient or group identifier was shared, and one image recurred (a lesion-free validation image that is also a fresh external case). Every threshold, selection rule and fitted quantity used training or validation data only.
% src: v5 P622, P624; RE117-125

The main test holds 507 cases from 416 patients or image groups, 219 with a lesion mask and 288 lesion-free. It gives 1,115 views: 219 each of original, erased and control-edited images, 288 lesion-free images and 170 degraded copies, for which the expected answer is a request for a better image. ``External centres'' denotes four public datasets from which no image was used in training (169 cases). Earlier prototypes of the trained model had been run on the main test and on the external centres. Two further sets were therefore assembled on which no model had been run when \ms{} was fixed: the fresh internal set (76 cases from ChestX-Det patients not used before) and the fresh external set (316 cases from four further datasets).

\subsection{Erased, control-edited and lesion-free views}
\label{subsec:views}

Each edit is defined by what it holds fixed and what it changes. The erased image changes the lesion and holds the rest of the image fixed: the lesion mask is dilated by 5 pixels and filled by the inpainting model LaMa \citep{suvorov2022lama}. The control edit holds the lesion fixed and changes only whether an edit was made: the same dilated mask shape is moved to a random tissue location that does not overlap it and is filled with the same tool, so no lesion pixel changes. It is the matched control for every property of the erasure except the removal of the lesion. Lesion-free images are unedited images of the same source. They carry the same expected answer as an erased image, so a model that answers ``absent'' only when it detects an edit fails on them.
% src: v5 P476-479

Training used two editing tools: LaMa and tissue transplant, in which tissue from the same image is blended over the lesion. Two tools were kept out of training and validation, the fast-marching method of Telea (TELEA) \citep{telea2004image} and Stable Diffusion inpainting \citep{rombach2022high}; main-test erasures and control edits were remade with each on the same masks. Because an erasure edits the lesion's site and the control edit another site, an \emph{edit-site control} was added on validation images: the tool fills a ring of the lesion's area that starts 2 pixels outside the lesion and leaves the lesion intact.
% src: v5 P482, P484; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (data); L1250-1255

Edited views passed automated checks of residual contrast, of unchanged lesion pixels in the control edit and of near-duplicates across splits; no view was selected by hand (Supplementary~\ref{sec:s_views}). These checks do not establish that a reader would call the lesion absent, which the reader study addresses. Three analyses reuse the main-test lesion cases. For the blank-image control, 183 cases were also shown as a uniform grey image, and as a lesion-free image of another imaging type, with the same question. \emph{Low-visibility erasures} are those that an edit detector scores at or below the median score of the control edits. Questions were classed as \emph{finding-specific} when they name a finding (114 of 219 cases) and as \emph{generic} when they ask about an abnormality or a normal appearance (105 cases).
% src: v5 P486; RE258-268, RE423-445; L1766, L1786

\subsection{Unedited image pairs}
\label{subsec:pairs}

REAL-Colon \citep{biffi2024realcolon} holds 60 complete colonoscopy videos from four centres, with a box on every frame in which a resected polyp is visible. For each of a video's three longest runs of boxed frames, a polyp frame was taken from the middle of the run and a polyp-free frame from the same video 10 to 30~s before the run (after it for 28 pairs), at least 10~s from any boxed frame. Polyp-free means that the dataset marks no box; the frames were not read again by an endoscopist. Both frames were shown with the question ``Is there a polyp in this image?''. This gave 103 pairs from 46 videos, a number set by the dataset and not by a power calculation. As the unedited analogue of the control edit, 96 pairs also received a \emph{context-matched} polyp-free frame from the same stretch of the examination, 3 to 10~s from the nearest boxed frame.
% src: v5 P604, P608; RE199-205, RE349-352; L1646

LDPolypVideo \citep{ma2021ldpolyp} holds 160 annotated colonoscopy clips from one hospital. No model of this study was trained, selected or tested on it before this analysis. The pairing rules were restated in frames, with one pair per clip, and 116 clips gave a pair.
% src: RE573-586; L2067-2070

Chest films were not paired by patient. Pneumothorax detection was tested on films with and without pneumothorax from 200 new SIIM-ACR patients \citep{siim2019pneumothorax} and on 600 test films of CheXthought \citep{sharma2026chexthought}, which records radiologists' statements and click points on CheXpert Plus images \citep{chambon2024chexpertplus}. The CheXthought labels combine the dataset label with a rule-based reading of the radiologists' statements; they do not come from a reader of this study. As a secondary analysis, follow-up pairs of the same patient were built from NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestx}. The rules for all pairs are given in Supplementary~\ref{sec:s_views}.
% src: RE493-500, RE518-520, RE206-210; L1370; L1964

\subsection{Outcome measures}
\label{subsec:measures}

Each final answer was classed as \emph{present} (the finding is reported), \emph{absent}, \emph{refer} (the image is usable but the model does not decide) or a request for a better image. The primary measure is the evidence sensitivity gap,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:esg}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{ESG} = {} & \Pr(\text{absent or refer} \mid \text{erased image}) \\
 & - \Pr(\text{absent or refer} \mid \text{control edit}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
ESG is zero for a model that always answers present, always answers absent or always refers, and for any model that responds to the two edits alike.
% src: v5 P490-498

\emph{Erased-lesion claims} are the share of erased images answered present, and \emph{lesion-free claims} the share of unedited lesion-free images answered present. \emph{Lesion images correct} is the share of original and control-edited images answered correctly. A model that always answers absent has no erased-lesion claims and no lesion image correct, which is why the two are read together.
% src: v5 P500

\emph{Net utility} is the mean reward per view under a cost matrix (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_cost}). A correct answer earns $+1$. A reported finding on an erased or lesion-free image costs $-2$, as does a wrong answer on a lesion image ($-4$ for findings marked in advance as critical, such as pneumothorax). Referral earns $+0.5$ on erased and lesion-free images and costs $-0.3$ on lesion images. The matrix was fixed on 30 October 2026, before any model reported here was trained, and was not elicited from clinicians. On the main test, always reporting the finding, always answering absent and always referring earn $-0.478$, $-0.848$ and $+0.110$.
% src: v5 P500, P513-515, P695-697

For unedited pairs, the \emph{natural ESG} is the share of lesion-free images answered absent or referred minus the share of lesion images of the same patients answered absent or referred. For chest films we report the \emph{Youden index} \citep{youden1950index}: the share of pneumothorax films answered present plus the share of films without pneumothorax answered absent or referred, minus one. Both are zero for any constant answer. The reference standard for a lesion is the expert mask or label of the source dataset; for an erased image it is absence by construction, which the reader study assesses. The primary outcomes are ESG, erased-lesion claims and net utility on the main test.
% src: v5 P606, P659; RE215-216, RE512-513

\subsection{Models}
\label{subsec:models}

Every model answered under one protocol. It received the image (long side at most 768 pixels), the question and the same system prompt (Supplementary~\ref{sec:s_prompts}). It could make up to three tool calls, to enlarge a region or to adjust contrast, and ended with an answer (with an optional box), a referral or a request for a better image.
% src: v5 P541

Nine comparison models were accessed through their providers' interfaces on 31 October 2026 with default settings: \texttt{gpt-6-sol}, \gpt{} and \texttt{gpt-6.1-sol} (OpenAI); \texttt{claude-fable-5-1}, \opus{} and \texttt{claude-sonnet-5-5} (Anthropic); and \gem, \texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} and \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash} (Google). Analyses that were not run on all nine used one model from each provider, \gem, \opus{} and \gpt, which we call the three reference models. Six open-weight models were run locally with greedy decoding: \base{} (the base of the trained models), MedGemma 1.5 4B \citep{sellergren2025medgemma}, Lingshu-7B \citep{xu2025lingshu}, HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B \citep{chen2024huatuogpt}, Qwen2.5-VL-7B \citep{bai2025qwen25vl} and Qwen3-VL-8B. For the open-ended request, the original and the erased image of 36 cases were shown with the request ``Describe any abnormal findings in this image'', without tools, and a text-only judge marked whether each reply reported the finding.
% src: v5 P659, P823; L450-455; RE270-278

\ms{} addresses what training on counterfactual views alone achieves. It is \base, an open-weight model of about 4 billion parameters, with a low-rank adapter (LoRA, rank 16) \citep{hu2022lora} on the language model; the vision encoder is frozen. Training examples were built automatically from the dataset labels. Training had three stages: supervised fine-tuning on 16,000 views (originals, erased images, control edits, lesion-free images, degraded copies and lesions pasted into lesion-free training images); fine-tuning on the model's own highest-scoring sampled answers; and fine-tuning on 5,000 views that mix these with questions from three public question-answering datasets \citep{lau2018vqarad,liu2021slake,he2020pathvqa}. The procedure, and the rule that chooses among the stages on validation data, were fixed on 1 November 2026, before the training data of this model were built. The selected model was run once on each test set, and no prompt or threshold was changed afterwards. \ms{} is the primary model.
% src: v5 P543-549; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (training, selection rule); facts A

\msp{} addresses whether adding a missing kind of image removes a miss. \ms{} found no pneumothorax on the fresh external set (Section~\ref{subsec:res_external}), and its first training stage had held four pneumothorax images. \msp{} repeats the three stages with the same data, settings and seed, and adds films of 150 patients with and 150 without pneumothorax from SIIM-ACR to every stage; none of these patients is in a test set. The model is post hoc. Its validation rule was fixed on 15 November 2026, before any of its test results.
% src: facts A (L788-790, RE129-136); L1368-1382

\msn{} addresses whether unedited training images bring detection on unedited images. It is \msp{} with two kinds of unedited image added to the last stage: 1,727 polyp frames and 1,881 polyp-free frames from REAL-Colon, and 1,500 films with and 1,500 without pneumothorax from CheXthought training patients. Each addition was first tested on its own (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_models}). \msn{} was designed post hoc, after those results and the results of \ms{} on unedited pairs were known. Its evaluation was pre-specified on 24 November 2026, before the model was trained and before any LDPolypVideo file was downloaded. Two training mixes were candidates: adding the images in full, which lowers the share of erased images in the last stage from 28.2\% to 9.9\%, or repeating erased images until that share is restored. A pre-specified rule on validation net utility chose the first (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_mix}). Because REAL-Colon then serves in training and in testing, answers on its pairs are \emph{cross-validated}: three copies of the model were each trained without one third of the videos, and each pair is answered by the copy that never saw its video.

The complete training of \ms{} and of \msn{} was repeated with two further seeds; the first seed is the reported model in each case. For \msn{} we also report a three-seed majority vote, the answer given by at least two of the three seeds, which costs three model runs per image.

Other routes to higher evidence sensitivity were tested as well, each against a criterion fixed in advance. Twelve alternative designs changed the training objective (preference optimisation \citep{rafailov2023dpo}, a consistency loss on paired views, reinforcement learning on the cost-matrix reward \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}), the adapter, the agent design or the mix of editing tools used in training, and 270 decision rules were applied to model outputs (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_plan}).

\subsection{Reader study}
\label{subsec:readers}

The specialist round is the primary reader assessment and was pre-specified. \emph{Images:} 212 main-test images, each from a different case: 36 each of unedited lesion images, LaMa erasures, LaMa control edits, unedited lesion-free images and Stable Diffusion erasures, and 32 Stable Diffusion control edits. \emph{Readers:} board-certified radiologists with at least five years of practice after certification, and a board-certified gastroenterologist for colonoscopy; none had taken part in the earlier round. \emph{Independence:} each reader worked alone through a personal link. \emph{What readers saw:} one image at a time, without metadata, in an order shuffled for each reader, with the clinical question of the case. \emph{What they were asked:} whether the image had been digitally edited, and the clinical question, answered yes, no or cannot tell. \emph{What they were told:} that the study concerns automatically edited medical images. They were not told which or how many images were edited, the kind of edit, the dataset labels, any model output or any other reader's answer. \emph{Criterion:} for each editing tool, the erasures are accepted when the share of erased images that the reader majority still calls present has an exact 95\% interval with an upper limit of at most 10\%. The gastroenterologist's answers count for colonoscopy images only. \emph{Closing rule:} the round closes when two radiologists have read all 212 images or on 10 January 2027, whichever comes first; the date cannot be moved once any answer has been examined, and with fewer than two radiologists the round is reported as incomplete.

An earlier round used two trainee physicians (internal medicine and radiology) and 128 main-test images, 32 each of unedited lesion images, LaMa erasures, control edits and unedited lesion-free images, with the same two questions and the same criterion. In a pre-specified analysis, stored model answers were scored again on the erasures that neither trainee called present (Supplementary~\ref{sec:s_readers}).

\subsection{Analysis plan and statistics}
\label{subsec:stats}

For \ms, the training procedure, the selection rule and the test sets were fixed on 1 November 2026, before its training data were built. For each later analysis the test set, the comparison and the decision rule were fixed and dated before the data they apply to were seen; we call such an analysis \emph{pre-specified}. An analysis fixed in advance without a decision rule is \emph{descriptive}, and everything else is \emph{post hoc}. The record is a set of dated files in the public project repository (\url{https://github.com/bhaskargurram-ai/medscope}), summarised analysis by analysis in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_plan}. It is the authors' own dated record and not a third-party registry. Four elements are post hoc and are labelled so where reported: the models \msp{} and \msn{} (their selection rules and tests were pre-specified); the family of 27 comparisons used for the multiplicity correction; the interaction analysis of the edit-site control; and the chest X-ray follow-up pairs, which were rebuilt after two faults in the first build.
% src: v5 P358, P980-989; facts B; L1443-1450, L1824-1832; L1280-1295

\emph{Primary comparisons:} \ms{} against each of the nine comparison models on the three primary outcomes, 27 comparisons, each tested at a Bonferroni-corrected level of $0.05/27$. \emph{Other comparisons:} two-sided at the 0.05 level without correction, read with their intervals. \emph{Intervals:} 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals from 2,000 resamples. \emph{Resampled unit:} the case on the edited test sets (all views of a case together), the video or clip for colonoscopy pairs and the patient for chest films. Resampling patients or image groups in place of cases widened intervals by at most 0.01 on the main test and 0.03 on the fresh external set. \emph{Comparisons between models:} paired, with both models scored on the same resampled units; the P value is twice the smaller share of resampled differences on either side of zero. \emph{Reader proportions:} exact binomial intervals. \emph{What an interval reflects:} the sampling of test cases for one trained model. Variation between training seeds is reported separately, as the mean and standard deviation over three seeds, and the two are never combined. \emph{Software:} Python with NumPy for the analyses; PyTorch 2.11, Transformers 5.22.1 and PEFT 0.21.2 for training and inference.
