\section{Analysis plan, models and methods in detail}
\label{sec:s_plan}

This section gives every analysis of the study with its status (Table~\ref{tab:s_overview}), the dated analysis plan (Table~\ref{tab:s_plan}), the evaluation sets in full (Table~\ref{tab:s_datasets}), the trained models (Table~\ref{tab:s_models}), the cost matrix (Table~\ref{tab:s_cost}), a bound on what a decision rule can add, the construction of the test views and pairs, the training details, further statistical details, the reader study protocol, and the prompts and model settings. Shares are given as percentages and differences between shares in percentage points; ESG, natural ESG, the Youden index, net utility and AUC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) are on their own scales.
% src: none (pointer)

\subsection{Overview of all analyses}

Table~\ref{tab:s_overview} lists every analysis of the study, in the order of the Results, with the design element that answers it, its test data, its status, the part of Table~\ref{tab:s_plan} that gives its decision rule, and where it is reported. An analysis is \emph{pre-specified} when its test set, comparison and decision rule were fixed and dated before the data it applies to were seen; \emph{descriptive} when its test set and comparison were fixed in advance without a decision rule; and \emph{post hoc} when it was added after earlier results were known. The plan was written during the study, from 30 October to 26 November 2026, each rule after the results that preceded it; each rule was written to a dated file in the public project repository (\url{https://github.com/bhaskargurram-ai/medscope}), whose file history is the record. It is our own record and not a third-party registry.
% src: v5 P358, P980; facts B; L229 (30 Oct, cost matrix and measures), L2121 (26 Nov, last results)

Confirmatory analyses are those in which no model of this study had been run on the test data when the rule was dated: \ms{} on the fresh internal and fresh external sets and on the REAL-Colon pairs, \msn{} on the LDPolypVideo pairs, the first test on the new SIIM-ACR patients (a variant of \ms{} with pneumothorax films in its last stage, 4 November 2026), and the two reader rounds. Four pre-specified analyses re-analyse main-test answers that existed, and whose pooled results were known, when the rule was fixed: the low-visibility erasures, the question types, the near-duplicate exclusion and the re-scoring on the trainees' answers; for these the rule was fixed before the analysis was run. Post hoc are: the models \msp{} and \msn, the last-stage variant and the two single-addition models (their selection rules and tests were fixed before they were trained or tested); the family of 27 primary comparisons; the interaction analysis of the edit-site control; the analysis of the pneumothorax failure of \ms{} (training coverage and image resolution); the plain prompt and the highest image-resolution setting; first-stage training with control edits only and with lesion insertions only; resampling of patients or image groups in place of cases; the comparison of image hashes across sets; the rebuild of the chest X-ray follow-up pairs; and a comparison of the comparator of the consistency loss with the first-stage model, reported with the alternative designs.

\subsection{A bound on what a decision rule can add}
\label{sec:s_bound}

A decision rule that acts on a fixed set of model outputs cannot create evidence sensitivity that those outputs do not contain. Let $S$ denote any signals computed from an image and its question, for example answer probabilities, confidence scores or the outputs of several agents, and let $\pi$ be any deterministic or randomised rule that maps $S$ to a final action. Let $\mu_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{c}}$ be the distributions of $S$ over erased images and over control edits. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:s_bound}
\mathrm{ESG}(\pi) \;\le\; \mathrm{TV}(\mu_{\mathrm{e}}, \mu_{\mathrm{c}}) \;=\; \sup_{A}\, \bigl|\mu_{\mathrm{e}}(A) - \mu_{\mathrm{c}}(A)\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
where TV is the total-variation distance and the supremum runs over sets of signal values. The statement follows from the data-processing inequality for total variation: the event ``$\pi(S)$ is absent or refer'' is a (possibly randomised) function of $S$, so the difference of its probabilities under $\mu_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{c}}$, which is the ESG of $\pi$, cannot exceed the distance between the two distributions.
% src: v5 P525-535

Two consequences were used in the study. First, a rule that refers more often can raise net utility, because referral is rewarded on erased and lesion-free images, without raising ESG. The 128 decision rules that recombine recorded outputs are of this kind. The 142 rules that add new evidence at test time (altered copies of the image, close-ups, a second model, answer-token probabilities) are not bound by the inequality for the original signals, which is why they were tested separately. Second, the right-hand side can be bounded from below: the hard decision of a linear probe on held-out cases is one event $A$, so its true-positive rate minus its false-positive rate is a lower bound on the distance. This is the measured bound of Table~\ref{tab:s_plan}; it is linear, and the true distance may be larger.
% src: v5 P537, P772, P812, P1041-1044; RE64-75

\subsection{Construction of the test views and pairs}
\label{sec:s_views}

\emph{Automated checks of edited views.} The contrast between the erased region and a surrounding ring of 6 to 14 pixels had to fall to 35\% of its value before the edit or less (or to 1.0, if that is larger), and an edit detector had to score the erased region below 0.5. A control edit could change no lesion pixel. Near-duplicate images across splits were removed by 64-bit difference hashing and pixel correlation. Views were filtered until a texture classifier separated erasures from control edits with an AUC of at most 0.60; the value on the main test was 0.56. Degraded copies were made by blurring, added noise, eightfold down-sampling and up-sampling, strong JPEG compression, motion blur, or substitution of an image of another imaging type.
% src: v5 P478, P486

\emph{Blank-image control, low-visibility erasures and question types.} The blank-image control uses the 183 main-test lesion cases whose yes/no question is answered ``yes'' on the original image. The blank view is a uniform mid-grey image of the same size. The swapped view is a lesion-free main-test image of another imaging type, chosen by a fixed hash of the case; it exists for 141 of the 183 cases, because swaps within one imaging type were removed after a coding fault. For the low-visibility analysis, a ResNet-18 was trained for five epochs to separate control-edited from unedited training views, both of which show the lesion, and then scored every main-test view. An erasure is low-visibility when its score is at or below the median score of the main-test control edits (111 of 219 erasures); existing answers were used and no model was run again. A question is generic when it asks about an ``abnormality'' or about a ``healthy'' or ``normal'' appearance, and finding-specific otherwise (114 and 105 lesion cases; 128 and 160 lesion-free cases).
% src: RE258-268, RE423-445; L1416-1417, L1766-1767, L1786-1788

\emph{REAL-Colon pairs.} A track is a run of boxed frames at least 1~s long, with gaps under 2~s merged. For each of a video's three longest tracks, the polyp frame comes from the middle half of the track, with the largest box at least 1\% of the frame. The paired frame comes from the same video, 10 to 30~s before the track starts and at least 10~s from any boxed frame; it was taken after the track when fewer than three frames qualified before it (28 of the 103 pairs; the written plan said ``no frame'', and the selection code fixed with the plan, which was followed, said ``fewer than three''). On each side, the sharpest of 12 evenly spaced candidates with a mean brightness of 40 to 215 was kept. The context-matched frame lies within 10~s of the ends of the track and 3 to 10~s from the nearest boxed frame, and was found for 96 pairs. Two limits were stated in advance: the two frames of a pair differ in more than the polyp (view, lighting, bowel preparation), and a paired frame may hold a small polyp without a box, because the dataset boxes the polyps that were removed.
% src: v5 P604, P608; RE199-205, RE232-234, RE349-352; L1646

\emph{LDPolypVideo pairs.} The dataset holds 160 clips from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University, with 40,186 labelled frames (33,875 with a polyp and 6,311 polyp-free); both of its parts were used, and nothing was trained on them. The clips state no frame rate, so the rules were written in frames, with one pair per clip. A track is a run of polyp frames at least 10 frames long with gaps of at most 5 frames, and the longest track of the clip is used. The polyp frame is chosen as for REAL-Colon. The polyp-free frame is marked polyp-free and lies at least 5 frames from any frame that is not. Of the 160 clips, 116 gave a pair; 39 had no polyp-free frame at least 5 frames from a polyp frame, and 5 had no polyp frame with a box of at least 1\% of the frame. The plan required at least 40 pairs for the decision rule to apply. The repository of the dataset states no licence, and its images are not redistributed.
% src: RE571-597, RE616-619; L2067-2070

\emph{Chest films.} CheXthought films are frontal views with the question ``Is there a pneumothorax in this chest X-ray?''. A film is positive when the CheXpert Plus label for pneumothorax is positive and at least one CheXthought radiologist states a pneumothorax, without expressing doubt, and marks it with click points. A film is negative when the label is negative or blank and no radiologist states or suspects a pneumothorax. A box counts as correct when its intersection over union with the box around the click points is at least 0.3. For the follow-up pairs from NIH ChestX-ray14, the lesion film is one whose only label is pneumothorax, pleural effusion, nodule or mass, and the paired film is the nearest study of the same patient and view position labelled ``No Finding'', with one pair per patient and up to 50 pairs per finding; the labels are mined from reports. The planned exclusion of patients in any ChestX-Det split matched no patient in either build. In the first build a check for near-copies wrongly removed 156 of 200 pairs, which left 44; the rebuild corrected that check and gave 200 pairs, of which 183 were scored. The rebuild is post hoc.

\subsection{Training details}
\label{sec:s_training}

\emph{Development versions.} Before the training procedure of \ms{} was fixed, the following versions of the trained model had been evaluated on the main test: an adapter trained only to refer; first-stage models with and without counterfactual views, at two seeds; a model after rejection-sampling fine-tuning; and that model continued with general question-answering data, which was the trained model of the earlier series. The last two were also run on the external datasets, and the earlier series was run with all four editing tools on 125 main-test cases, after which tissue transplant was added to training. The training of \ms{} was changed in response to weaknesses these versions showed: false findings on lesion-free images, a regression on SLAKE CT and MRI, lower detection of real lesions at the external datasets, weak chest X-ray and small-lesion results, and a possible dependence on one editing tool. The record does not give the number of versions run.
% src: facts A ("Before v4"); L343-356 (first look: final RFT model and refer-only adapter on the main test), L459-477 (first stage with and without counterfactual views; continuation with question-answering replay), L617-674 (second seed), L541-604 (external datasets), L698-715 (four tools, 125 cases; transplant added), PREREGISTRATION_v4.md "Why a v4" (weaknesses)

\emph{Data preparation.} Training used 1,508 SLAKE and colonoscopy cases and 1,792 chest X-ray, breast ultrasound and brain MRI cases, with 427 and 690 lesion-free images; the validation set holds 540 cases (1,473 views). Excluded before splitting were 179 of 642 SLAKE masks (grey levels not listed in the mask key), 623 diffuse chest X-ray findings and 179 lesions outside the size limits in ChestX-Det, 88 BUSI lesions too large to erase, and 364 TCGA-LGG slices with little brain. The lesion-free colonoscopy images are 427 HyperKvasir frames of normal landmarks (caecum, retroflexed rectum and ileum). Every patient or image group that holds a main-test image was removed from the training and validation splits of \ms.
% src: v5 P620, P624

\emph{Adapter and training examples.} The adapter is a low-rank adapter (LoRA) \citep{hu2022lora} of rank 16 ($\alpha = 32$, dropout 0.05) on all linear layers of the language model; the vision encoder is frozen. Each training example is a sequence of turns built from the labels alone: a \texttt{zoom} call on a jittered box around the lesion (always when the lesion covers less than 2\% of the image), a \texttt{window} call on 30\% of greyscale images, and then the expected final action with short template reasoning. Half of the training questions were reworded with four paraphrases; three further paraphrases were kept for testing.
% src: v5 P543; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (training)

\emph{Stages.} Stage 1 is supervised fine-tuning on 16,000 views for one pass (3,706 optimiser steps) at a learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-4}$. In stage 2, four answers per view are sampled at temperature 1.2, the successful answer with the highest reward is kept, views that the model mostly failed are repeated three times, and the model is fine-tuned on these at a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$. Stage 3 is fine-tuning at the same learning rate on 5,000 views that mix counterfactual views with questions from the training splits of VQA-RAD, SLAKE and PathVQA; views whose expected answer is ``absent'' make up at least 35\% of the mix. Seeds change the initialisation of the adapter, the order of the data and the random choices in the training examples; the seeds of a model share their training data. For the three-seed vote of \msn, the answer of each seed on a view is classed as present, absent, refer or other (a request for a better image, or an output that cannot be parsed). The vote is the class that two or three seeds chose. When all three differ, a referral is taken if one seed refers, and otherwise a request for a better image.
% src: v5 P545-547, P659; PREREGISTRATION_v4 (training, reporting rule)

\emph{Hardware and software.} Each stage of \ms{} took about 4 hours on one NVIDIA A100 graphics processor with 40~GB of memory, with PyTorch 2.11, Transformers 5.22.1 and PEFT 0.21.2. The final runs of \msn{} used PyTorch 2.14.1, Transformers 5.24.1 and PEFT 0.22.0 on several graphics processors; on an A100 with 80~GB, two jobs at a time, the last stages of its two candidate mixes took 5 and 6.5 hours.
% src: v5 P659 (MedScope-4B: about 4 h per stage, A100 40 GB; software); report60 L4-7 (software of the MedScope-4B-N runs); L2080-2084 (5 h and 6.5 h on an A100 80 GB; mixed GPUs)

\emph{Pneumothorax films in \msp.} The SIIM-ACR patients were divided at random, with a fixed seed, into training patients (150 with and 150 without pneumothorax), validation patients (98 cases, 242 views) and the 200 new patients used for testing; none is in the fresh external set.
% src: PREREGISTRATION_v4 (post-hoc note 2); RE129-131; L2104, L2112

\emph{Unedited images in \msn.} From every REAL-Colon video, polyp frames were drawn from up to ten tracks: one frame per 2~s of track, at most six per track, with the largest box at least 0.5\% of the frame. About as many polyp-free frames were drawn per video: near a track by the rule of the test pairs, context-matched, or at least 30~s from any boxed frame; a video without tracks gave twelve. This yielded 1,727 polyp frames, 772 of them with a polyp under 2\% of the frame, and 1,881 polyp-free frames. CheXthought films are frontal views only. The zoom box of a positive film is the bounding box of the radiologists' click points, padded by 5\% of the image on each side and at least 15\% of the image in width and height. Patients were divided by a fixed hash into a training pool (80\%) and a test pool (20\%); training used 1,500 positive and 1,500 negative films and testing 300 and 300, at a long side of at most 768 pixels. CheXpert Plus images and CheXthought annotations are used under their research-use agreements and are not redistributed.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:s_stats}

The $p$ value of a paired comparison is twice the smaller share of the 2,000 resampled differences on either side of zero, so the smallest non-zero value it can take is 0.001; a value of zero is printed as $p < 0.001$. Resampling patients or image groups in place of cases (post hoc; 416 groups on the main test, 155 at the external datasets, 75 in the fresh internal set and 230 in the fresh external set) widened intervals by at most 0.01 on the main test and 0.03 on the fresh external set. All 27 primary comparisons kept $p < 0.001$, and one other comparison crossed 0.05: fresh-external ESG of \ms{} against \opus, $p = 0.037$ with cases and $p = 0.060$ with patients resampled. Outside the 27 primary comparisons, $p$ values are not corrected for multiplicity and are read with their intervals.

\subsection{Reader study protocol}
\label{sec:s_readers}

\emph{Trainee round.} Two trainee physicians, one in internal medicine and one in radiology (recorded as one year each), read 128 main-test images: 32 each of unedited lesion images, LaMa erasures, LaMa control edits and unedited lesion-free images. The images were shown one at a time in random order on a web page without labels. For each image the reader answered whether the image appeared to be artificially edited, and whether the finding named in the clinical question was present (yes, no or cannot tell). Readers gave no reasons. They had no access to the labels, the kinds of edit or each other's answers. The criterion, fixed before any answer was collected, was that the upper limit of the 95\% interval of the share of erased images with the finding reported is at most 10\%.
% src: v5 P792, P1552; facts E; L991-994, L1153, L1166; READER_VERIFIED.md L3; RESULTS_SUMMARY L110-111

\emph{Re-scoring of model answers on the trainees' answers.} This analysis was fixed on 14 November 2026, before any model was scored in this way. An erasure is \emph{clean for both readers} when neither trainee answered that the finding was present, and \emph{called present by both} when both did. For every model run on the main test, the existing answers on these images were used; no model was run again. The nine comparison models were pooled, with a bootstrap over images (2,000 resamples). The rule is in Table~\ref{tab:s_plan}.
% src: READER_VERIFIED.md

\emph{Specialist round.} The round was fixed on 15 November 2026. The set was planned as 216 images, 36 of each of six kinds; 212 were used, because only 32 Stable Diffusion control edits were available from distinct cases. Radiologists were required to be board certified with at least five years of practice after certification, and a gastroenterologist or endoscopist was sought for colonoscopy. Each reader received a personal link with an opaque code. The invitation stated that the study evaluates whether automatically edited medical images still show a finding, that the 212 images come from public research datasets and carry no patient information, that each image has two questions (whether it has been digitally edited, and the clinical question shown), and that answers should rest on the image alone. Consent to the use of the answers under a reader code was recorded on the page before the first image. Readers recorded their specialty, whether they are board certified and the years since certification, and whether they had taken part in the earlier round. Each answer was saved as it was given, and the latest answer per image counts. The order of images was shuffled for each reader. An optional free-text note could be added to any image.

The closing rule was fixed on 18 November 2026, before any answer was examined: the round closes when two board-certified radiologists have read all 212 images, or on 10 January 2027, whichever comes first; the date is not moved after any answer is seen; and with fewer than two radiologists the round is reported as incomplete, with what was collected. The gastroenterologist read all 212 images.
