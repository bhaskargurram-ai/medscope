Two models appear here that the main text does not name: each of the two kinds of unedited training images in \msn{} was first added to \msp{} on its own, giving ``\msp{} with colonoscopy pairs'' and ``\msp{} with CheXthought films'' (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_models}). Unless a caption says otherwise, brackets are 95\% confidence intervals (CI) from $2{,}000$ bootstrap resamples, differences are paired over the same cases, videos or patients, $p$ values are two-sided, and a bootstrap $p$ value of $0.000$ is printed as $<0.001$. Claims are printed as percentages and their differences as differences in proportions, as recorded. The three training seeds of \msn{} are called first, second and third; the first is the model of the main text. n.r.: not recorded (where the record says so, the interval includes zero). --: not applicable or not computed.

\label{subsec:s_pairs}

Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s_realcolon} to~\ref{tab:s_ldpolyp} give every model on the REAL-Colon pairs, on the context-matched frames, with one centre withheld from training, and on LDPolypVideo.
% src: pointers only, no numbers

\paragraph{Where \ms{} missed polyps (descriptive)} On the 103 polyp-free REAL-Colon frames \ms{} reported a polyp on 2 (\base{} 15, \opus{} 20) and asked for a better image on 12, of which 11 came from one centre; this is why $86.4\%$ (89 of 103) of its polyp-free frames count as answered ``absent'' or referred. On the polyp frames it called the polyp present on 36 and asked for a better image on 3. By thirds of polyp box area (small, middle, large) it called 5 of 34, 7 of 35 and 24 of 34 present; \base{} 5, 8 and 27; \opus{} 19, 23 and 31. The median polyp box covered $2.9\%$ of the frame, and 35 of 103 covered less than $2\%$. \ms{} made no tool call on 61 polyp frames (5 called present, 53 ``absent'', 3 requests for a better image) and one zoom on 42 (31 called present, 11 ``absent'').

\subsection{Counterfactual views, unedited pairs and alternative designs}
\label{subsec:s_designs}

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_twobytwo} gives the two-by-two comparison of training images. The seventeen alternative designs of Section~\ref{subsec:res_designs} are the twelve of a first series and five tested later. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_designs} lists the twelve and the 270 decision rules, Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s_designs_obj}, \ref{tab:s_designs_cons} and~\ref{tab:s_designs_tools} give their complete results, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_designs_later} three designs tested later, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_inspect} the other two, which rewarded inspection, and Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s_designs} the changes against each comparator.
% src: pointers only; the counts twelve and 270 are from v5 P767, P772; seventeen = 12 rows of tab:s_designs + 3 of tab:s_designs_later + 2 of tab:s_inspect

\paragraph{The designs in words} Preference optimisation is direct preference optimisation \citep{rafailov2023dpo} on pairs of a preferred and a rejected answer built from lesion removal. The consistency loss asks for the same decision score on an original view and its control edit and for a margin between the control edit and the erasure. Reinforcement learning is group relative policy optimisation \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} on the cost-matrix reward. The multi-agent system combines the trained model, a copy that does not receive the question, a lesion segmenter, a verifier that erases the reported region, makes an equal edit elsewhere and asks again, and a deciding agent that reads their reports; in the first design the deciding agent was trained on reports for training images, in the second on reports for validation images. Of the 270 decision rules, 128 combine the outputs of these agents by thresholds, regularised logistic regression, boosted trees and referral bands. The 142 rules with test-time evidence add three altered copies of the image (mirror, centre crop, contrast), a close-up of the reported region with an equal-area control close-up, a repeated question with a cautious prompt, the probability of the first answer token, a second model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and the segmenter score. Among the 128 rules that combine agent outputs, the largest gain in net utility on the second half of the validation set came with erased-lesion claims of $3.9\%$ against $11.2\%$ and lesion images correct of $0.727$ against $0.773$.
% src: v5 P553-567 (designs), P1043-1044 (rules); L1127-1205

\paragraph{Continued training as a comparator (post hoc)} The comparator of the consistency loss, continued training on the same views with no new objective, itself reported fewer erased lesions with tools not used in training than the first-stage model in two of three seeds ($-0.080$ and $-0.085$, $p < 0.001$; $-0.012$, interval includes zero) and had a higher ESG with these tools in one ($+0.076$, $p = 0.001$; $+0.022$ and $+0.027$, intervals include zero), with fewer unedited lesion images correct at one seed ($-0.049$, $p < 0.001$). This comparison was not planned in advance.
