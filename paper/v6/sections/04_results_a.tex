\subsection{Answers of the comparison models often persisted after erasure}\label{subsec:res_main}

On the main test (507 cases, 1{,}115 views), the nine comparison models reported the finding on $41.1\%$ (\gem) to $61.6\%$ (\texttt{gemini-3.8-flash}) of the 219 images from which the lesion had been erased, with ESG between $0.269$ and $0.470$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a).
They were correct on $82.9\%$ to $88.8\%$ of lesion images, so models that differed by less than 6 percentage points in lesion images correct differed by about 20 percentage points in erased-lesion claims.
The share of erased lesions reported is not a clinical error rate: an erased image is an edited image, and whether readers accept it as lesion-free is the subject of Section~\ref{subsec:res_readers}.
On the 288 unedited lesion-free images the comparison models reported a finding on $9.0\%$ to $30.9\%$.
% src: v5 P663 and P676-686 (0.829-0.888 printed as percentages; 41.1-61.6; ESG 0.269-0.470; 9.0-30.9); L484-493; "less than 6" and "about 20" restate the two ranges (round-2 brief, decision 4; referee E.3 item 3)

The question alone did not produce these reports.
When the image of 183 lesion cases was replaced by a uniform grey image, none of the nine models reported the finding, and each asked for a better image.
On the erased images of the same cases they reported it on $42.1\%$ to $56.8\%$ (all $p < 0.001$ against the blank image; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_blank}).
What in the erased image led to the reports (surrounding tissue, the instrument, residual lesion or the edit) is examined in Sections~\ref{subsec:res_readers} and~\ref{subsec:res_alternatives}.

\subsection{Reader assessment of the erased images}\label{subsec:res_readers}

Two board-certified specialists read all 212 images of the specialist round without labels: a radiologist (8 years of practice) for brain MRI, breast ultrasound, chest X-ray and SLAKE images, and a gastroenterologist (20 years) for colonoscopy (Table~\ref{tab:readers}).
Further radiologists are reading, and the results below are interim.
Of the 36 erasures made with LaMa, the tool of the main test, the specialist for the imaging type accepted 20 as free of the finding (15 with an explicit ``no'') and called the finding present on 16.
On the 20 accepted erasures the nine comparison models still reported the finding on $43.3\%$ (95\% CI $31.7$ to $56.1$; 5 to 12 of 20 per model), and on $37.8\%$ ($23.7$ to $53.3$) of the 15 with an explicit ``no'' (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_specialist}).
\ms{} and \msn{} each reported it on 1 of 20, $38.3$ percentage points less often than the comparison models on the same images (95\% CI $22.8$ to $52.8$), and found 59 and 61 of the 66 lesions that the specialists confirmed.
The lower bound of $31.7\%$ is above the $20\%$ set for this analysis, the bound used for the trainee round, so the persistence holds on erasures that specialists accepted.
% src: L2237-2248 (R29: 20 accepted, 15 strict, 16 called present; 43.3% (31.7-56.1); 37.8% (23.7-53.3); 5/20 to 12/20; 1/20 each; 59/66 and 61/66; +38.3 [+22.8, +52.8]; lower bound 31.7% > 20%); L2272-2281 (radiologist 8 years; 16/36); L2209-2215; docs/REVIEW_EXPERIMENTS.md Amendment 10 (R29 rule, written before the analysis ran)

The specialists did not accept every erasure.
The radiologist called the finding present on 13 of 29 LaMa erasures ($44.8\%$; exact 95\% CI $26.4$ to $64.3$) and 16 of 26 Stable Diffusion erasures ($61.5\%$; $40.6$ to $79.8$) of radiology images, and on 5 of 29 lesion-free images; the gastroenterologist on 3 of 7 LaMa and 1 of 10 Stable Diffusion erasures of colonoscopy images, and on 0 of 7 lesion-free images.
Neither separated edited from unedited images well (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, AUC, $0.64$ and $0.68$; for the radiologist, erasures against control edits $0.53$).
The pre-specified criterion (upper limit at most $10\%$ for each tool) was not met.
On the 16 erasures a specialist called present, the comparison models reported the finding on $64.6\%$ ($44.4$ to $83.4$) and \ms{} on 3.
% src: L2272-2281 (radiologist on radiology sources; AUC 0.64 and 0.53; criterion); L1385-1394 and R5_interim_R1.md L4-18 (gastroenterologist 3/7, 1/10, 0/7, AUC 0.68); L2237-2248 and L2283-2297 (64.6% (44.4-83.4); MedScope-4B 3/16)

In an earlier round, two trainee physicians each read 128 main-test images and did not separate edited from unedited images (AUC $0.54$ and $0.55$).
They called a finding present on 35 of 64 reads of erased images ($54.7\%$) and on 23 of 64 reads of lesion-free images ($35.9\%$).
Both did so on 13 of 32 erased images ($40.6\%$; exact 95\% CI $23.7$ to $59.4$), so the pre-specified criterion was not met.
The rates on erased and on lesion-free images were close in colonoscopy (3 of 12 against 2 of 12 reads) and in SLAKE CT and MRI (5 of 12 against 6 of 12, where half of the lesion-free reads were also called present); in the other three sources the erased rate was higher (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_trainee}).
% src: L1153-1166 (by source: colonoscopy erased 1/6 + 2/6, lesion-free 2/6 + 0/6; SLAKE erased 4/6 + 1/6, lesion-free 4/6 + 2/6; final_numbers N1), L1692-1695; v5 P787 (3/12 against 2/12), v5 P792 (AUC 0.54 of the first trainee; 40.6% [23.7, 59.4]); facts E

Where both trainees called the finding present (13 erased images), the nine comparison models together reported it on $82\%$ of images; where neither did (10 images), on $26\%$ (95\% CI $10\%$ to $42\%$), and the pre-specified lower bound of $20\%$ was not met (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_rescoring}).
Part of the erased-lesion claims therefore fell on images that readers did not accept as lesion-free, and part on images that the specialists did accept.
Two of the four readers who have finished are co-authors; all read without labels.

On the main test \ms{} reported the finding on $9.6\%$ $\ci{6.1}{13.7}$ of the 219 erased images and had an ESG of $0.758$ $\ci{0.701}{0.815}$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a).
Its ESG exceeded that of each comparison model by $0.288$ $\ci{0.208}{0.367}$ (\gem) to $0.489$, and its erased-lesion claims were lower by $31.5$ to $52.1$ percentage points.
On the main test this result did not come from answering ``absent'' more often: \ms{} was correct on $84.9\%$ of lesion images, against $82.9\%$ to $88.8\%$ for the comparison models (differences $-3.9$ to $+2.1$ percentage points, all $p \ge 0.12$).
The lower limits reach $-9$ percentage points, so a small deficit is not excluded.
On the 288 unedited lesion-free images it reported a finding on $7.6\%$ $\ci{4.8}{10.9}$, a lower share than eight comparison models and not shown to differ from \gem{} ($9.0\%$; $p = 0.59$).
Net utility, a measure that favours the trained model (Section~\ref{subsec:measures}), was higher by $0.168$ to $0.477$.
All 27 primary comparisons had $p < 0.001$, below the corrected level of $0.05/27$, and every interval excluded zero; the smallest differences were those from \gem: ESG $0.288$ $\ci{0.208}{0.367}$, erased-lesion claims $-31.5$ percentage points $\ci{-38.5}{-24.3}$ and net utility $0.168$ $\ci{0.083}{0.254}$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_primary}).

The advantage in ESG and erased-lesion claims held in point estimate against each reference model in each of the five sources (ESG from $0.95$ in colonoscopy to $0.47$ in SLAKE CT and MRI; Fig.~\ref{fig:main}b), although in SLAKE CT and MRI its net utility ($0.22$) was below that of \gem{} ($0.38$) and \opus{} ($0.32$).
On the 114 cases whose question names the finding, the ESG of \ms{} was $0.728$ $\ci{0.649}{0.807}$, against $0.298$ to $0.439$ for the nine comparison models (smallest difference $0.289$ $\ci{0.175}{0.412}$), and the pre-specified criterion was met.

\subsection{Detection of the edit does not account for most of the result}\label{subsec:res_alternatives}

\ms{} was trained on erased and control-edited images built by the same procedure as the test, so it might answer ``absent'' whenever an image looks edited (Table~\ref{tab:alternatives}, Fig.~\ref{fig:tools}).
Edits can be detected: a classifier trained for the purpose separated control-edited from unedited test images with an AUC of $0.826$.
% src: L1416-1417 (AUC 0.826)

\textit{Matched control edit.}
A model that responds only to the edit has an ESG of zero, because ESG subtracts the share of control-edited images on which the lesion is denied (answered ``absent'' or referred).
\ms{} denied the lesion on $14.6\%$ of the 219 control-edited images and on $90.4\%$ of the 219 erased images.
Control edits also mattered in training.
Among first-stage models, one trained with erasures only denied $43.2\%$ of 125 intact lesions after a control edit made with TELEA, a tool not used in training; with control edits added (a post hoc arm) the figure was $16.0\%$, and with all views $23.2\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:tools}b).
The control thus reduced the effect of an unfamiliar tool and did not remove it.
% src: v5 P888 (14.6%, 90.4%; derived there from ESG and the erased-lesion claim rate); v5 P737 and P1209-1218, L828-834 (43.2%, 16.0%, 23.2%)

\textit{Amount of lesion removed.}
Answers followed how much of the lesion was removed.
With LaMa, the first-stage model denied $4.2\%$ of 143 validation lesions after a distant control edit, $7.7\%$ after a ring edit at the lesion border, $7.0\%$ when the lesion pixels were pasted back into the erased image, $19.6\%$ when half of the mask was erased and $86.7\%$ when all of it was (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_site}).
% src: v5 P1305 and P778 (0.042, 0.077, 0.070, 0.196, 0.867); L1269-1271

\textit{Tools not used in training.}
With the erased and control-edited images of 125 main-test cases remade on the same masks, the ESG of \ms{} was $0.752$ and $0.720$ with the two training tools (LaMa and tissue transplant) and $0.664$ and $0.640$ with TELEA and Stable Diffusion, above each reference model with each tool (Fig.~\ref{fig:tools}a).
The smallest advantage was over \gem{} with TELEA ($0.664$ against $0.560$), and these twelve comparisons are exploratory.
A development version of the model, trained with LaMa edits only, had been run with all four tools, after which tissue transplant was added to training (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_tools}).
With Stable Diffusion \ms{} reported $17.6\%$ of erased lesions, against $7.2\%$ with LaMa ($p = 0.006$).
% src: v5 P739 and P1234-1237 (ESG and claims by tool); L636-637 (development version trained with LaMa only), L700-715 (run with the four tools; tissue transplant then added to training); L757-761 (Stable Diffusion +10.4 points, p 0.006)

\textit{Edit-site control.}
Erasures lie on the lesion and control edits away from it, so an edit at the lesion site might itself change the answer.
Like the analysis above, this control was run on the first-stage model and validation cases, not on \ms{} and the test set.
With LaMa a ring-shaped edit around the intact lesion raised denial from $4.2\%$ to $7.7\%$ (difference $3.5$ percentage points $\ci{-2.1}{9.1}$); with TELEA the model denied $20.3\%$ after the ring edit against $12.6\%$ after the distant edit (difference $7.7$ percentage points $\ci{0.0}{15.4}$), which met the pre-specified flag for a site effect.
In a post hoc comparison with \base{} on the 112 cases found by both, the excess of ring over distant edit was $1.8$ percentage points $\ci{-8.0}{11.6}$ larger for the trained model; the interval is too wide to exclude a site effect of the size the flag was set for.
Training about doubled denial after a TELEA edit at either site ($12.5\%$ against $6.2\%$ at the ring; $8.9\%$ against $4.5\%$ away from it; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s_site} and~\ref{tab:s_logodds}).
% src: L1269-1271, L1278-1290 (flag met with TELEA; +0.077 [0.000, 0.154] and +0.018 [-0.080, 0.116] printed in percentage points; 12.5 / 6.2; 8.9 / 4.5); v5 P778, v5 P1305-1308 (LaMa ring minus distant +0.035 [-0.021, 0.091], printed in percentage points)

\textit{Erasures that an edit detector scores as unedited.}
An erasure was called low-visibility when a trained edit detector scored it no higher than the median control edit (111 of 219).
On these the ESG of \ms{} was $0.640$ $\ci{0.550}{0.721}$, higher than that of each reference model (all $p < 0.001$; differences in Table~\ref{tab:alternatives}), and the pre-specified criterion was met.
\ms{} reported $16.2\%$ of the low-visibility erasures against $2.8\%$ of the other 108: either some ``absent'' answers follow edit traces or the least visible erasures retain more of the lesion, which this design cannot separate (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s_visibility}).
% src: L1416-1423; facts C10

Taken together, the answers of \ms{} followed mainly whether the lesion region had been erased, with some sensitivity to unfamiliar edit traces; the test without any edit is in Section~\ref{subsec:res_pairs}.
